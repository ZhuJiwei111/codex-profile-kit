# Revision judgment

## Evidence order

Judge a proposal against live evidence in this order:

1. Canonical paper source and its real include/import chain.
2. Verified data, result summaries, code-observed behavior, figures, tables, and
   authoritative citations.
3. Reviewer/editor concern and venue requirements.
4. Author intent and explicitly locked choices.
5. Historical tasks, old drafts, and task boards as motivation only.

Separate observed facts, manuscript interpretation, inference, recommendation,
and author decision. Never promote a planned or candidate result to completed
evidence.

## Verdicts

- `必须修改`: a factual, numeric, methodological, citation, or figure/text
  contradiction; or a required concern remains unanswered.
- `建议修改`: the source materially harms accuracy, argument, or readability,
  and the proposal has a concrete benefit.
- `不建议修改`: the proposal lacks a real benefit, creates defensive prose,
  repeats existing content, or is mere manuscript churn.
- `作者选择`: multiple evidence-compatible alternatives express different
  emphasis or tradeoffs that belong to the author.
- `作者锁定风险`: a contradiction is verified, but the author explicitly
  chooses not to change it. Keep the risk visible and unresolved.

## Contribution and claim boundary

Revision is not synonymous with narrowing. Preserve a strong contribution when
direct evidence supports it. Narrow only the words that cross the evidence
boundary, such as causal mechanism, universal superiority, unseen-domain
generalization, or exclusivity not isolated by the study.

Prefer a precise positive claim over a vague disclaimer. Do not add caveats for
imagined objections, list every control in narrative prose, or describe launcher,
debugging, revision, and audit history in the paper. Reproducibility facts belong
in Methods or Supplement when readers need them; project history does not.

For rebuttal-driven revisions, answer the actual concern and keep the manuscript
result-driven. Do not turn Introduction, Results, or Discussion into a checklist
of reviewer requests. Do not volunteer weak, negative, ambiguous, or unrelated
findings when they neither answer the concern nor alter the intended claim. If a
negative result changes the claim, adjust the claim honestly.

## Required judgment record

Every proposal must state:

- `verdict`;
- `direct evidence` and its location;
- `necessity` of the proposed change;
- `consequence if unchanged`;
- `claim boundary` after the change;
- `contribution preserved`; and
- any `residual risk`.

Avoid generic reasons such as "clearer", "more academic", or "more robust".
Name the ambiguity, contradiction, reader inference, or unsupported claim that
actually changes the decision.

## Approval boundary

Treat author feedback as evidence, not blanket permission. A confirmation
approves the current exact package only. An annotation approves or rejects only
the selected text. A new candidate invalidates approval of the old candidate
until the author confirms the replacement.
